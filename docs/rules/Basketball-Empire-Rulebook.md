# Basketball Empire

## Player Rulebook

**Players:** 2–4  
**Recommended players:** 4  
**Game type:** Online, turn-based basketball strategy board game  
**Recommended game length:** About 60 minutes

## 1. The Goal

In Basketball Empire, you are a basketball team manager building a league empire. Sign teams, complete divisions, recruit star players, create championship lineups, and earn points when opponents visit your teams.

At the end of the game, the player with the greatest total asset value wins.

Your final value may include:

- Available points
- Signed teams
- Star players
- Championship lineups
- Transport routes
- Training centers
- Complete-division bonuses

## 2. Joining a Game

One player creates a room and shares its invitation code. A room supports two to four players.

When joining, enter a player name containing 1–16 characters. Your full name appears in player panels, trades, game history, and the final rankings.

The game also creates an avatar from your initials:

- `Aaron` becomes `A`.
- `Aaron Wang` becomes `AW`.
- `Aaron James Wang` becomes `AW`.
- A Chinese name uses its first two characters.

Players with the same initials are distinguished by their player colors.

After joining, select **Ready**. The host may start the game when everyone is ready. Turn order is selected randomly.

## 3. Starting the Game

Each player begins with:

- 1,500 points
- One playing piece
- No teams or other assets

All teams, transport routes, and training centers begin unsigned. Shuffle the Team Operations and Game-Time Moments decks.

Every playing piece begins on **Tip-Off**.

## 4. Taking a Turn

On your turn:

1. If you are on the Bench, first attempt to leave it.
2. Roll two dice.
3. Move clockwise by the total rolled.
4. Resolve the space where you land.
5. Use the management phase to recruit stars, trade, mortgage assets, or remove mortgages.
6. Select **End Turn**.

A normal turn has a 45-second timer. Decisions that appear after movement normally have a 20-second timer. If you do not answer in time, the game applies the safest default action, such as declining an unsigned team or rejecting a trade.

### Rolling doubles

If both dice show the same number, complete the turn and roll again. If you roll doubles three consecutive times, go directly to the Bench.

## 5. Tip-Off

Whenever you pass or land on **Tip-Off**:

- Collect 200 points.
- Increase your completed-lap count by one.

If you are sent directly past Tip-Off by a penalty, you do not collect the reward unless the effect specifically says otherwise.

## 6. Signing Teams

The board contains 22 fictional basketball teams divided into eight colored divisions.

### Landing on an unsigned team

When you land on an unsigned team, you may sign it for the price shown on its Team Card.

- Select **Sign Team** to pay the listed price and take control of it.
- Select **Decline** to leave it unsigned.
- If the decision timer expires, you automatically decline.

There are no auctions. If you decline, no other player can purchase that team during your turn. It remains available when someone lands there later.

### Landing on an opponent's team

Pay the team owner the Game Revenue shown on the Team Card. The amount depends on the team's current lineup level.

You do not pay if:

- The team is mortgaged.
- A card effect cancels the payment.
- The team belongs to you.

## 7. Completing a Division

Teams with the same color belong to one division. A division contains either two or three teams.

If you control every team in a division:

- The base Game Revenue for its undeveloped teams is doubled.
- You may recruit Star Players for teams in that division.

If control of the complete division is broken, you may not recruit additional stars until the division is complete again.

## 8. Recruiting Star Players

Star Players improve a team's Game Revenue. They serve as lineup upgrades and are represented by Star markers or cards.

The development path is:

> Base Team → 1 Star → 2 Stars → 3 Stars → 4 Stars → Championship Lineup

The recruitment cost and resulting Game Revenue are shown on each Team Card.

### Balanced development

Teams in the same division must develop evenly. The difference between the number of stars on any two teams in that division cannot be greater than one.

For example, every team in a three-team division must have at least one star before any of them can receive a second star.

Once a team has four stars, you may pay its next upgrade cost to replace those stars with a Championship Lineup.

### Releasing stars

You may release stars back to the game for half their recruitment price. Stars must also be released evenly across their division.

## 9. Transport Routes

There are four Transport Route spaces. A player may sign an available route after landing on it.

When an opponent lands on one of your routes, collect revenue according to the total number of routes you control:

| Routes controlled | Revenue |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 25 points |
| 2 | 50 points |
| 3 | 100 points |
| 4 | 200 points |

Transport Routes cannot recruit Star Players.

## 10. Training Centers

The board has two Training Centers:

- Offensive Training Center
- Defensive Training Center

When an opponent lands on one of your Training Centers:

- If you control one center, collect four times the opponent's dice total.
- If you control both centers, collect ten times the opponent's dice total.

Training Centers cannot recruit Star Players.

## 11. Team Operations Cards

When you land on a **Team Operations** space, draw the top card and follow its instructions. These cards usually concern team finances, management, sponsorships, and lineup changes.

Possible effects include:

- Receiving sponsorship income
- Paying training or community-event costs
- Collecting points from other players
- Moving a star within a complete division
- Keeping a Bench Pass for later use

Unless a card says to keep it, return it to the bottom of the deck after resolving it.

## 12. Game-Time Moments Cards

When you land on a **Game-Time Moments** space, draw the top card and follow its instructions. These cards create unpredictable basketball events.

Possible effects include:

- Advancing to another team or Transport Route
- Moving backward
- Receiving points for a buzzer-beater
- Paying a technical-foul penalty
- Going directly to the Bench
- Rolling for a three-point reward
- Challenging another player in overtime
- Doubling the next revenue collected by one of your teams

Resolve any destination space unless the card says otherwise.

## 13. The Four Corner Spaces

### Tip-Off

Collect 200 points when passing or landing here and record one completed lap.

### Bench / Just Watching

If you land here normally, you are only visiting and may continue normally on your next turn.

If an effect sends you to the Bench, you must leave it before moving normally.

### Locker Room

Nothing happens. This is a safe resting space.

### Ejected!

Go directly to the Bench. Do not collect a Tip-Off reward, and end your turn immediately.

## 14. Leaving the Bench

At the start of a Bench turn, choose one of these options:

- Pay 50 points, then roll and move normally.
- Use a Bench Pass card, then roll and move normally.
- Roll the dice and attempt to get doubles.

If you roll doubles, leave the Bench and move using that roll. You do not receive an additional roll for those doubles.

You may attempt to roll doubles for up to three turns. If the third attempt fails, pay 50 points and move using that roll.

While on the Bench, you may still collect revenue, trade, mortgage assets, and manage your lineups.

## 15. Trading

During the management phase, the current player may offer a trade to another player.

A trade may include:

- Teams
- Points
- Transport Routes
- Training Centers

The receiving player may accept, reject, or make a counteroffer. A trade offer expires after 30 seconds. The current player may begin no more than two trades during one turn.

A team with Star Players or a Championship Lineup cannot be traded. Its upgrades must first be released according to the balanced-development rule.

Future promises and real-world money are not valid parts of a trade.

## 16. Mortgaging Assets

You may mortgage an undeveloped team to receive the mortgage value shown on its card.

- A mortgaged team collects no Game Revenue.
- A team with stars or a Championship Lineup cannot be mortgaged.
- To remove a mortgage, pay the mortgage value plus 10%.

Transport Routes and Training Centers follow the mortgage instructions shown on their cards.

## 17. Running Out of Points

If you owe more points than you have, you may attempt to raise the amount by:

- Releasing Star Players
- Mortgaging assets
- Trading with another player

If you still cannot pay, you are bankrupt.

- If you owe another player, your transferable assets go to that player.
- If you owe the game, your assets return to the game and become available again.

In elimination mode, a bankrupt player leaves the game. In a lap-based game, bankruptcy also removes that player from further turns.

## 18. Disconnecting and Returning

If you disconnect, your seat and game state are reserved for three minutes. During that time, the game uses safe automatic choices for your turns.

After three minutes, an AI player may temporarily take control. If you return, you regain control of your original name, avatar, points, teams, and board position.

## 19. Pausing the Game

Any player may request a pause.

- In a four-player game, at least three players must agree.
- In a two- or three-player game, more than half the players must agree.
- Each player may request up to two pauses per game.
- A pause lasts no longer than three minutes.

## 20. Ending the Game

The host selects the game length before play begins:

| Mode | Laps per player | Approximate time |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Quick | 4 | 30 minutes |
| Standard | 6 | 60 minutes |
| Long | 8 | 90 minutes |
| Elimination | No lap limit | Until one player remains |

In a lap-based game, the ending is triggered after every active player has completed the selected number of laps. Finish the current round so that all players have taken the same number of turns.

The game then calculates each player's total asset value from:

- Available points
- Unmortgaged teams
- Transport Routes
- Training Centers
- Half the recruitment value of Star Players
- Championship Lineups
- Complete-division bonuses
- Any outstanding mortgage costs

The player with the greatest total is the winner of **Basketball Empire**.

## 21. Tiebreakers

If two or more players have the same total value, compare in this order:

1. Number of teams controlled
2. Number of Championship Lineups
3. Available points
4. One final dice roll

Build your divisions, recruit your stars, and create a basketball empire.
