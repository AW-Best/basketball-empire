    A YEAR 8 PROJECT

    How I Made
    Basketball Empire

    I am 14 and I am in Year 8. This is the story of how an idea I had for a basketball board game became a real website that my friends could play.

    OPEN WITH"I started with no finished game. I only had an idea that sounded fun."
    02

    WHERE IT STARTED

    Basketball plus a board game

    I like basketball, and I also like games where people buy places, make deals and try to finish with the most money. I wondered what would happen if those two things were put together.

    Instead of buying streets, players could own basketball teams. Instead of building houses, they could recruit star players. That was the first version of the idea.

    The name came later: Basketnopoly, a mix of basketball and Monopoly.

    03

    MAKING IT PLAYABLE

    The theme was easy.
    The rules were harder.

    I had to decide what every part of the board meant. Teams became the main properties. Game Time and Team Operations became card spaces. Routes and training labs had their own prices and payments.

    01 Roll two dice 02 Move block by block 03 Buy or auction a team 04 Recruit better players 05 Trade with your friends 06 Finish with the most PTS

    I changed the rules many times after playing. A rule can sound fine on paper and still feel unfair in a real game.

    04

    TURNING WORDS INTO CODE

    How I worked with Codex

    I used Codex to help write and change the code. I did not give it one giant instruction and get a finished game. I worked feature by feature.

    "Let four players join the same room."
    "Move the piece one block at a time after the dice stop."
    "Show who owns the team, not only the word owned."

    The more exact my instructions became, the better the result was. I still had to decide what the game should do, test it and point out what felt wrong.

    05

    THE PART PEOPLE DO NOT SEE

    A lot of things broke

    Some bugs were obvious. A button did nothing. A phone could not connect. The wrong player became champion. Other bugs were strange, like the game history appearing on top of the auction screen.

    BUG 01 Failed to create a room BUG 02 Phone connection closed BUG 03 Recruit button did nothing BUG 04 History covered the bid controls

    For each bug, I tried to describe exactly what I clicked, what I expected and what happened instead. Screenshots helped a lot. Fixing bugs took more time than making some of the first features.

    06

    FROM MY LAPTOP TO MY FRIENDS

    Getting the game online

    At first, the game only worked on my laptop at a localhost address. That was useful for testing, but nobody at another house could join.

    I used GitHub to save the code and its history. I first shared the running game through Tailscale Funnel. I also learnt how a hosting service can keep a website online when my laptop is turned off.

    The multiplayer test was simple: create a room on one device, join on another, then check that both screens showed the same turn.

    07

    GIVING IT AN ADDRESS

    Why buy a domain?

    A temporary server link is difficult to remember. A domain gives the game a proper web address that I can say in class or send to a friend.

    Buying the domain is only one step. I also need to connect its DNS records to the place where the game is hosted, turn on HTTPS and test that the address works on both Wi-Fi and mobile data.

    TYPE THISbasketball-empire.[domain]DOMAIN → DNS → HOSTING → GAME

    I learnt that a domain is the address. The server is the computer that actually runs the game.

    08

    AFTER PEOPLE ARRIVE

    Checking daily visits

    Once the site was online, I wanted to know whether anybody was using it. I added a visit counter and looked at the daily visits instead of guessing.

    The useful questions are quite basic: How many people opened the site today? Did they use a phone or computer? Did they reach the game, or leave on the first page?

    I do not need people's real names for this. A simple anonymous count is enough to see whether a change helped.

    09

    WHAT I WOULD TELL ANOTHER STUDENT

    Start with the smallest version

    I did not know every step when I began. I made one screen, then one room, then one working turn. Each problem showed me what I needed to learn next.

    Have an idea you care about.Explain one feature clearly.Test it with real people.Keep the parts that are fun.

    Basketball Empire is still not finished. That is probably why I still enjoy working on it.
